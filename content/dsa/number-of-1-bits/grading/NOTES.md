# Grader notes: Number of 1 Bits

The classic version passes a 32-bit pattern too, and its current form allows only `1 <= n <= 2^31 - 1`. Here every `int` is a valid input: Java has no unsigned `int`, so a pattern whose bit 31 is 1 arrives as a negative number, and the statement says so. Calling `Integer.bitCount` or `Long.bitCount` is forbidden by the statement only; no test can tell, and a solution that calls them passes. The answer is a single `int`, so there is only one correct output.

Tests cover: the sample (1000 gives 6; -256, whose top 24 bits are 1, gives 24; -2147483648, only bit 31, gives 1); 0 and small patterns (6, 7, 255, 1023, 4096); every single-bit pattern, bit 31 included; every run of 0 to 32 ones at the bottom and at the top of the word, which includes the pattern of 32 ones; negative patterns written in hex, such as `0x80000001`, `0xFFFF0000`, `0xAAAAAAAA` and `0xF000000F`, and -999; large positive patterns (`0x7FFF0000`, `0x55555555`, `0x0FFFFFFF`, `0x3FFFFFFF`, `0x40FFFF00`); all 65,536 patterns of the low half alone, of the high half alone and of the low half under bit 31; and 100,000 seeded random patterns. The bulk tests check against the test's own count, which reads the 32 positions one at a time. The six small tests have a 1-second limit, so that a solution that loops forever fails quickly; the sample is first and has a negative input. The two bulk tests, `everyPatternOfEachHalf` and `randomPatterns`, make about 300,000 calls and have the default 5-second limit, because a correct solution through strings needs a large share of a second there: counting the '1' characters of the padded or unpadded text took 217 to 551 ms on the halves and 83 to 147 ms on the random patterns on an idle machine, and the review measured up to 900 ms with other test JVMs running.

The traps the walkthrough and hints name, each run as a scratch variant: shifting with `>>` until the value is 0 times out on the 6 tests with a negative input; `while (x > 0)` counts nothing for a negative input and fails the same 6; adding `x % 2` fails the 4 tests with a negative odd input; `Integer.toString(x, 2)`, which writes a minus sign and the magnitude, fails 5; indexing all 32 characters of `Integer.toBinaryString` fails 7, on every positive input with fewer than 32 digits; and 32 minus the number of '0' characters fails 7, since leading zeros are not written. Two more were run: `Math.abs` before counting and dividing by 2 instead of shifting each fail 5. The walkthrough's three approaches pass, and so do shifting with `>>>` until the value is 0, a fixed 32 shifts with `>>`, and the parallel count of the second follow-up.

No naive solution: time is not graded separately for this question (ADR 0008). One call is a constant amount of work for every correct approach, at most 32 steps of the Better, 32 characters of the Brute force or 15 operations of the parallel count, so no correct approach is slower by a factor a timed test could separate. The reference took 10 to 12 ms on the 196,608 patterns of the halves and 6 to 9 ms on the random patterns, over six cold runs.
