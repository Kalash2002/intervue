# Grader notes: House Robber II

Tests cover: the sample; a single house (5, and 0), which the split into two streets would turn into 0 if not handled first; two houses in both orders; three houses, all neighbours of each other (a straight-street answer of 9 fails, the answer is 6); `[10,1,1,10]`, where the two 10s touch around the circle (the straight-street answer 20 fails); `[1,5,1,1,9]`, where the answer needs the last house (always dropping the last house gives 6 and fails); 200 seeded random circles of 1 to 14 houses checked against a search over every allowed set in the test; and two roads at the size limit: 1,000 houses of 1,000,000 (answer 500,000,000) and 1,000 houses of 5, 1, 1 repeated with a final 5 next to house 0 (answer 1,666; the straight-street answer, 1,670, fails).

The two large tests have a limit of 1,000 ms each (3x is 3,000 ms). The reference, two straight-street passes with two running totals each, finishes each in well under a millisecond. The naive solution is the same split with the plain take-or-leave recursion on each street, whose calls grow by a factor of about 1.6 per house; it times out on both large tests, also at 3x. Its recursion is at most 1,000 deep, so it times out rather than overflowing the stack. The walkthrough's memoised recursion is O(n) like the reference and passes.
