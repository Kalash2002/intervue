# Grader notes: Meeting Rooms

Deviation from the classic version: up to 100,000 bookings (the classic allows 10,000) with minutes from 0 to 10^9, so that comparing every pair can be timed out. The setting is the coworking space of Meeting Rooms II, with new values. As in the classic, a booking that ends at the minute another starts does not clash with it; the statement says so and the tests check the boundary both ways (touching shares the room, an overlap of one minute does not). The answer is a yes or no, so there is only one correct output.

Tests cover: the sample (Example 1 is a booking inside another that no pair of neighbours in the given order shows, Example 2 touches twice and fits, Example 3 is two bookings with the same start); touching bookings sharing the room, also out of order (comparing with `<=` fails this, the sample, the random test and the first timed test); an overlap of one minute, in both orders; a booking inside another, in both orders; a clash between bookings four places apart in the list (`[20,22]` and `[21,25]`; checking only neighbours in the given order, the trap the statement and the Level 1 hint name, fails this, the sample, the random test and the second timed test); no bookings, one booking and one booking over the whole range of minutes; two identical bookings; 300 seeded random lists of 0 to 8 bookings, half of them built one after another with gaps of 0 to 2 minutes and then shuffled, checked in the test against every pair; and two timed lists of 100,000 bookings.

The first timed list is 100,000 bookings that each end as the next starts, listed as the even ones in order and then the odd ones from the last back, so it fits. The second is bookings `[10i, 10i + 5)` in order, except that booking 70,000 is moved inside booking 30,000. That is the only clash, between positions 30,000 and 70,000 of the list and 30% of the way through in time, so a pair search finds it only after about 2.5 billion comparisons whichever end it starts from and whichever way its inner loop runs. The timed lists are not shuffled so that the reference's sort stays cheap and the limit can stay tight; order cannot help the pair search, which never sorts.

Both timed tests have a limit of 400 ms (3x is 1,200 ms). The reference, sort by start and compare neighbours, took 18 to 33 ms on the first and 5 to 9 ms on the second over six cold runs, most of it building the list. The naive solution is the walkthrough's brute force, which compares every pair; it does not finish the first list in 4 s and takes about 3 s on the second. Its three mirrors (the outer loop from either end, the inner loop forwards or backwards) take 2.7 to over 4 s on the second list and time out on the first. The walkthrough's Better, two sorted columns, is O(n log n) like the reference and passes, as does sorting by end and comparing neighbours.
