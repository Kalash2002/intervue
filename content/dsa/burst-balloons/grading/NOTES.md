# Grader notes: Burst Balloons

The rules are the classic ones: 1 to 300 balloons numbered 0 to 100, a burst pays its number times the numbers of its nearest unburst neighbours, a missing neighbour counts as 1, and the method returns the largest total. A burst pays at most 10^6, so the answer is at most 3 × 10^8, and every partial total in the table is the pay of some of the bursts, so it is no larger; the statement says the answer fits an `int`. Several orders can pay the most; the method returns only the total, so no tie rule is needed.

Tests cover: the sample (`[2, 3, 7, 4, 2]` 133, `[5, 0, 7]` 42, `[9]` 9); the first two rows reversed; a row where neither bursting the smallest first (128) nor always bursting the one that pays most now (222) is best (`[2, 6, 5, 3, 4]` 240, burst 3, 5, 6, 2, 4), and its mirror; zeros (`[0, 8, 0, 6, 0]` 56, five zeros 0, a single 0); the largest numbers (`[100, 100, 100]` 1,010,100); and 300 seeded random rows of 1 to 7 balloons, numbered 0 with chance 1 in 5 and otherwise 1 to 100 (167 rows hold a zero), checked against a search in the test over every order of bursting. The seed was chosen so that no random row is a classic sample row or one edit away from one.

The three timed tests have 300 balloons each. Three hundred 100s give 298 × 10^6 + 10^4 + 100 = 298,010,100: a burst pays at most 100^3, the last one has no neighbours and pays at most 100, the one before it has at most one neighbour and pays at most 100 × 100, and bursting the inner balloons first reaches all three bounds. A hundred and fifty 100s with a 0 after each give 148,010,100: bursting every zero first never lowers another burst, since a burst whose neighbour was a zero paid 0 and every other burst keeps the same neighbours, so the answer is that of 150 hundreds in a row. One 100 among 299 ones gives 30,000: no burst can pay more than 100, and bursting the neighbours of the 100 one at a time, then the 100, gets 100 every time.

Traps, each checked with a scratch solution: bursting the smallest first fails 8 of 9 tests and always bursting the biggest pay first fails 7, the sample among them for both (70 and 130 on Example 1); splitting the row at the first balloon burst, with its neighbours from the original row, fails all 9 (196 on Example 1); counting a missing neighbour as 0 fails all 9 (0 for `[9]`); the right rule filled row by row with `lo` going up, which reads cells not yet filled, fails all 9 (65 on Example 1).

The timed tests have a limit of 500 ms each (3x is 1,500 ms). The reference, the table filled by the length of the stretch, took 18 to 32 ms on the first (it includes the warm-up of the triple loop), 6 to 12 ms on the second and 5 to 17 ms on the third over cold runs. The naive solution is the walkthrough's brute force, which tries every balloon as the next one; it is still running after 5,000 ms (10x) on all three. Its recursion is at most 301 deep, which fits the stack. The walkthrough's Better, the memoised recursion over stretches, is O(n³) like the reference and passes every test, also interpreted (`-Xint`); its recursion is at most 301 deep too.
