# Grader notes: Merge Triplets to Form Target Triplet

The classic version merges two triplets at a time, writing the result over one of them, and asks whether the target ever appears. This one states the same thing as overlaying any non-empty set of presets: merging one triplet into another again and again produces exactly the component-wise maxima of the sets of triplets merged, so the answers agree. Sizes are the classic ones (up to 100,000 presets, levels 1 to 1,000). The answer is `true` or `false`, so no tie rule is needed.

Tests cover: the sample (Example 1 needs three presets and must leave out one that overshoots green; in Example 2 the only red 35 comes with blue 50; Example 3 is a single preset one short); overlaying every preset overshoots (`[60,40,80]` with `[90,30,20]` for target `[60,40,80]`, and a target needing two of three presets while the third is `[90,90,90]`; overlaying everything, the wrong move the walkthrough and Level 1 hint name, fails 4 of the 10 tests, the first timed test among them); a level on a preset that overshoots does not count (two cases, false; finding each target level somewhere in its channel, also named, fails 4, the second timed test among them); a level equal to the target fits (`[40,90,20]` and `[40,30,60]` for `[40,90,60]`, true; a filter that wants every other level strictly below the target fails 5); several presets needed (three and two); levels below the target cannot rise (false); one preset (`[1000,1000,1000]` and `[1,1000,1]` true, `[1000,1,1000]` for `[1000,2,1000]` false; looking only for a preset equal to the target fails 6); 300 seeded random desks of 1 to 8 presets with levels 20 to 24, half with the target set to the overlay of a random non-empty set so the answer is true, checked in the test against every non-empty set; and two timed desks of 100,000 presets around the target `[700,400,900]`. In both, half the presets fit under the target but reach none of its levels, and half have one target level but go above the target in another channel. In the first, presets 40,000, 50,000 and 60,000 fit and supply red, green and blue: true. In the second, presets 40,000 and 50,000 supply red and green, but every preset with blue 900 overshoots, preset 60,000 by one on green: false. Every preset and target in the tests differs from every triplet of the classic samples in at least two channels (checked by a script), so none is one edit from a classic one.

Both timed tests have a limit of 400 ms (3x is 1,200 ms); the limit is that wide because building 100,000 small arrays takes 15 to 30 ms on a cold JVM, more than the pass itself. The reference, overlaying every preset that fits, takes 28 to 47 ms on the first and 16 to 24 ms on the second, nearly all of it building the input. The naive solution is the walkthrough's brute force, every non-empty set counted off by a binary counter; it never finishes, because every set that works on the first desk holds presets 40,000, 50,000 and 60,000, which a counter from either end reaches only after more than 2^40,000 sets, and the second desk has none. The counter run from the other end times out on both as well. The slower variant the walkthrough names on the way, every choice of up to three presets, is O(n³): its first preset alone has about 5 × 10^9 pairs to try with it, and it times out on both, whether its loops run from the front or from the back. The walkthrough's Better, one fitting preset per channel, is O(n) like the reference and passes.
