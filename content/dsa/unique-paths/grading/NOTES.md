# Grader notes: Unique Paths

The rules are the classic ones: `1 <= rows, cols <= 100`, moves right or down, and the answer is at most `2 * 10^9`, so it fits an `int`. Every count the tables build is the count for a cell between the start and the goal, and those counts only grow towards the goal, so no intermediate value passes the answer. Only one answer is possible, so no tie rule is needed.

Tests cover: the sample (4 by 5 is 35, 6 by 4 is 56, a single cell is 1); one row or one column, up to 100 long (1); two rows or two columns (50 by 2 and 2 by 50 give 50); a floor and its transpose (9 by 13 and 13 by 9 give 125,970); every floor from 1 by 1 to 10 by 10, checked against a count in the test of every sequence of moves with the right number of downs; two narrow floors at the largest length (100 by 5 gives 4,421,275, which is 103 choose 4, and 6 by 100 gives 91,962,520, which is 104 choose 5); and two floors near the largest count allowed: 16 by 20 gives 1,855,967,520 (34 choose 15) and 69 by 8 gives 1,984,829,850 (75 choose 7).

The closed form named in the follow-up answers, a running product for the binomial coefficient, fails the two large floors when it multiplies in an `int` (it returns 90,783,155 and 144,129,580): the product just before the last division is k times the answer. Multiplied in a `long` it passes everything.

The two large floors have a limit of 200 ms each (3x is 600 ms). The reference, one row of counts, finishes each in under a millisecond. The naive solution is the walkthrough's brute force, which follows the parcel from the start and makes about two calls per route; it takes about 7.5 s and 10.7 s on the two large floors, past the limit and 3x. Its recursion is at most 198 deep, so it times out rather than overflowing the stack. It passes every other test, the 6 by 100 floor in about 0.6 s. The walkthrough's Better, a full table of counts, is O(rows × cols) like the reference and passes; so does the closed form in a `long`, which is faster still but shows no difference at this size.
