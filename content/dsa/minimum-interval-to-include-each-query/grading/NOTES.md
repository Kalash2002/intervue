# Grader notes: Minimum Interval to Include Each Query

Deviation from the classic version: posts run from 1 to 10^9 (the classic stops at 10^7); the sizes, up to 100,000 zones and 100,000 points, and the rules are the classic ones: closed zones, a size that counts both end posts, -1 for an uncovered point, answers in the order of the points. The largest size, 10^9, fits an `int`. Each answer is a number, so there is only one correct output per point.

The tests compare the returned array element by element after checking it is not null and has one answer per point, and say which point is wrong.

Tests cover: the sample (Example 1 has a point on a zone's last post, an uncovered point, and a point where the zone that starts nearest it is not the smallest; Example 2 has a point one past a zone's end and a repeated point; Example 3 is a zone of one post); both end posts covered and the posts just outside (`[20,24]` at 20, 24, 19, 25); the size counting both ends (`[[63,63],[63,64]]`, sizes 1 and 2; `to - from` fails 11 of 12 tests); answers in the order of the points, with repeats (answering in sorted order, or sweeping the points in the given order, fails 7); a small zone that has ended before a point while a large one still covers it (`[[10,11],[5,40]]` at 11 and 12: never throwing away ended zones fails 11, and answering -1 when the top has ended fails 9); a zone that starts later and is smaller (`[[1,50],[30,31]]`); four nested zones; no zone covering any point; posts at 1 and 10^9; 300 seeded random lists of 1 to 8 zones and 1 to 8 points, checked in the test against every zone; and two timed lists. Ordering the heap by end instead of size fails the limits test and the random test; taking the covering zone that starts nearest the point, the trap the walkthrough names, fails the sample and the random test; treating the last post as uncovered fails 8.

The first timed list has 99,999 small zones `[10k + 1, 10k + 5]` and one zone `[1, 1,000,000]` in the middle of the list, with 100,000 points in increasing order, alternately inside a small zone (answer 5) and in the gap after it (answer 1,000,000, the only zone covering it). The second has 100,000 zones nested around post 100,000, zone k covering 100,000 - k to 100,000 + k, and 100,000 points at every distance from 0 to 99,999: first the even distances, to the right and outwards, then the odd ones, to the left and outwards; the answer for distance d is 2d + 1. The points come in two runs because sorting 100,000 shuffled points with a comparator took the reference to 167 to 228 ms on a cold JVM, above an eighth of the limit; in two runs it takes under 120 ms. The slower approaches do not sort the points, so the order does not help them.

The limits are 600 ms on the first timed test (3x is 1,800 ms) and 1,500 ms on the second (3x is 4,500 ms), which fills the heap with every zone. The reference, the sweep with a heap of open zones, took 23 to 29 ms on the first and 86 to 118 ms on the second over six cold runs. The naive solution is the walkthrough's brute force, every zone for every point: 14.2 s on the first (still running after 15 s in the last run) and 30.5 s on the second. The walkthrough's Better, zones by size with the first cover winning, takes 11.7 s and 8.0 s, because the gap points scan every small zone before the wide one and each nested point scans d + 1 zones; with ties in size broken the other way it takes 14.8 s and 5.7 s. The brute force with both loops run backwards takes more than 15 s and 20.6 s, and a version that binary-searches the zones that have started and scans all of them takes 3.7 s and 13.9 s.
