# Grader notes: Word Break

The sizes are the classic ones: up to 300 letters and 1,000 different words of 1 to 20 letters.

Tests cover: the sample; a longest-first piece that leads into a dead end (`pinkink` with `pink`, `pin`, `kink`; Example 1 as well); a shortest-first piece that does (`cartridge` with `car`, `cart`, `ridge` and `notebook` with `no`, `note`, `book`; shortest-first also fails `abcabcab` with `abc`, `ab`); words used again (`murmurmur`, `abcabcab`); a tail no word fits (`doors`, `kayaks`, false); words that run past the end of the message (`plan` with `pl`, `anet`, false, and `tea` with `teapot`, `t`, `ea`, true; a table that compares `text.substring(start, start + word.length())` throws on these and on most other tests); a one-letter message; 300 seeded random messages of 1 to 12 letters over `a` and `b` with 1 to 5 words of 1 to 3 letters, checked against a search in the test over every set of cut points; a 288-letter message, `seatidecartridge` 18 times, that splits as sea tide cart ridge although `seat` and `car` lead into dead ends (both greedy rules fail it); and two 300-letter messages that cannot be split. The first is 149 `a`s, a `b` and 150 `a`s with the words of 1 to 20 `a`s plus `bb` and `bab`: the piece holding the one `b` would need a word with exactly one `b`, and there is none. The second is 150 `a`s, a `b` and 149 `a`s with the same 20 runs of `a` and 980 seeded random words that each hold a `c`, which the message lacks, for 1,000 words in all.

The two messages that cannot be split have a limit of 500 ms each (3x is 1,500 ms). The reference, a table of reachable positions, finishes them in about 1 and 7 ms. The naive solution is the plain search, every fitting word and then the rest; it tries every way to cut the run of `a`s before the `b` and is still running after 5 seconds (10x) on both. Each `b` sits in the middle of its message and both vocabularies contain the letter `b`, so a search from the back, or one that first checks that every letter of the message appears in some word, times out the same way; so does a memoised search that remembers only the positions that succeed. The walkthrough's memoised search is O(n × W × L) like the reference and passes, as do a table over a hash set of the words and a memo keyed by the remaining text.
