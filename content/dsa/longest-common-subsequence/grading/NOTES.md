# Grader notes: Longest Common Subsequence

The rules are the classic ones: two strings of 1 to 1,000 lowercase letters, return the length only. The length is a single number, so no tie rule is needed even when several subsequences are longest.

Tests cover: the sample (`cordon`/`condor` 4, `pistachio`/`captain` 4, `golf`/`flog` 1); the sample pairs with the arguments swapped; two pairs where pairing each letter with its earliest copy in the other string finds 2 instead of 4, whichever string leads (`sterile`/`latrine`, `coaster`/`orchestra`); a word against its reverse with six different letters (`repaid`/`diaper`, 1); repeated letters (`llama`/`mall`, 2, with four letters shared); 300 seeded random pairs of 1 to 10 letters over `ab` or `abc`, checked against a search in the test over every subsequence of the first string, which also produces pairs with nothing in common and pairs where one string is a subsequence of the other; and two pairs at the size limit. The first is 300 `a`s then 700 `b`s against 600 `b`s then 400 `a`s: a common subsequence cannot mix the letters, since each string has them in the opposite order, so the answer is 600. The second is 1,000 seeded letters from `a` to `m` against a copy with every tenth letter replaced by one from `n` to `z`: the 100 replaced letters match nothing, and the other 900 are a subsequence of the original, so the answer is 900.

Traps, each checked with a scratch solution: earliest-copy greedy fails the sample, the swapped sample, the greedy test, the random test and the opposite-runs test (300), and taking the better of both directions still fails the first four; the longest common substring fails the sample, the swapped sample, the greedy test, the random test and the replaced-letters test (9); counting shared letters fails every test but one.

The two large tests have a limit of 500 ms each (3x is 1,500 ms). The reference, two rows of the table, takes 25 to 33 ms on the first (it includes the warm-up of the table loop) and 8 to 13 ms on the second. The naive solution is the walkthrough's brute force, which pairs equal first letters and otherwise tries dropping each; it is still running after 6 s on both, past the limit and 3x, because both inputs keep the letters differing for long stretches. Its recursion is at most 2,000 deep, so it times out rather than overflowing the stack. The walkthrough's Better, the full table, and a memoised version of the brute force are O(n × m) like the reference and pass.
