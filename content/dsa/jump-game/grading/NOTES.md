# Grader notes: Jump Game

The row may hold up to 100,000 platforms (the classic version allows 10,000) so that the O(n²) table from the end, which the walkthrough names as its Better, can be timed out; springs go up to 100,000, as in the classic. The answer is true or false, so no tie rule is needed.

Tests cover: the sample; the longest jump leading to a dead end (`[2,3,0,0,1]` and `[4,0,3,0,0,0]`, both true; always taking the longest jump, the wrong greedy the walkthrough names, says false here and on Example 1); a zero that is jumped over (`[3,0,0,1,0]`) and a zero on the last platform (`[1,0]`), both true, which a solution that gives up at any zero gets wrong; a strong spring on a platform that cannot be reached (`[1,0,5,0]` is false; the farthest reach taken over every platform, without checking that the platform itself is within reach, says true, and also fails the random test, the untimed long rows and the first timed row); one platform (`[0]` and `[7]`, true); landing exactly on the last platform (`[3,0,0,0]`, `[2,0,0]` true; `[2,0,0,0]` false); 300 seeded random rows of 1 to 12 platforms with springs 0 to 4, checked against trying every sequence of jumps in the test; two rows of 100,000 springs of 1, the second with a 0 at platform 70,000 (true, then false; untimed); and two timed rows of 100,000. In the first, platforms 0 to 59,999 all land at most on platform 60,000, which has no spring, and every platform after it could reach the end: false, with the dead end in the middle of the row, so a search from the last platform backwards also has 40,000 platforms to look through. In the second, platform 0 reaches the last platform directly, and platforms 1 to 99,997 all land at most on platform 99,998, which has no spring: true.

The two timed rows have a limit of 150 ms each (3x is 450 ms). The reference, the farthest reach, finishes each in under 20 ms on a cold JVM, usually 3 ms. The naive solution is the walkthrough's Better, the table from the end that scans each platform's landing platforms until it finds a good one; it takes 1.2 to 1.5 s on the first row and 3.2 to 3.7 s on the second, past the limit and 3x. It is the naive rather than the brute force because the brute force recursion is one call deep per jump: on these rows it overflows the stack (a `StackOverflowError`, also on the untimed rows of weak springs) instead of timing out. Both fail the timed tests, and so do the other O(n²) forms tried: the table scanning each range from the far end, marking every platform reachable from each reachable one (with or without stopping once the last platform is marked), a breadth-first search, and the mirrored table that asks, from the last platform backwards, which platforms can be reached from platform 0. The goal-moving pass from the end is O(n) and passes.
