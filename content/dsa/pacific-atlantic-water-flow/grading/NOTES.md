# Grader notes: Pacific Atlantic Water Flow

The cells returned are sorted by row, then column, before they are compared, so any order is accepted. Tests cover: the sample, water crossing equal heights (a 2 by 2 plateau, and a ring of 3s around a pit: a version that needs a strictly lower step strands most of the ring), a single row, a single column and a single cell, the two corners that touch both oceans (`[[1, 9], [9, 1]]`), a pit and a cell that can drain only into it (a version that floods from the oceans downhill instead of uphill fails), twenty seeded random maps of up to 10 by 10 with heights 0 to 4, whose expected answers the test computes with one downhill search per cell and ocean, and three 200 by 200 maps.

The large maps: a basin of 0s walled in by 100,000s one cell in from the border, so only the outer two rings (1,584 cells) drain anywhere; a slope that rises along a path snaking down the rows, where every cell below the top row drains to both oceans and the top row drains only left, except its right-hand corner (39,801 cells); and a level plain of 7s, where every cell drains to both and a recursive depth-first search runs 40,000 calls deep and overflows the call stack.

The limit for each large test is 1,000 ms (3x is 3,000 ms). The reference, two breadth-first searches climbing from the edges, took 4 to 7 ms on the basin, 37 to 44 ms on the winding slope and 23 to 25 ms on the plain, on a cold JVM. The naive solution searches downhill from every cell: it timed out on the basin even with the limit raised twentyfold (20,000 ms), since each of the 38,416 basin cells searches the whole basin, and it timed out on the winding slope at the limit.
