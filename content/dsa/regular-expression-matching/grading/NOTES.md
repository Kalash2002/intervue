# Grader notes: Regular Expression Matching

The pattern language is the classic one, defined exactly in the statement: lowercase letters, `.` for any one letter, and `*` after a letter or a dot for any number of copies of that piece, none included; a star never starts the pattern and never follows another star; the match must cover the whole text. Two things differ from the classic version, both stated in the constraints: the text and the pattern may each be up to 100 characters long, so that the backtracking search can be timed, and either may be empty. Every test pattern is valid under that definition. The answer is `true` or `false`, so no tie rule is needed.

Tests cover: the sample (`color`/`colou*r` true, `seventeen`/`s.*en` true, `banana`/`ban*` false, the empty text with `x*y*` true); a starred piece matching no letter at the start, in the middle and at the end (`x*lamp`, `la.*mp`, `lampy*`, and `e*ok*xq*` for `ox`); a starred piece that must leave letters for what follows (`pinpoint`/`p.*nt`, `beef`/`be*ef`, `toot`/`to*ot`, all true); a star that repeats only the piece before it (`haha`/`ha*` and `bandana`/`ban*.` false, `tool`/`to*l` true); the whole text (`pinpoints`/`p.*nt`, `stars`/`st.r`, `star`/`stars` and `upstart`/`star`, all false); a dot is one letter (`cart`/`c.t` false, `cot`/`c.t` true, the empty text with `.` false); `.*` mixing letters (`road`/`r.*d`, `kayak`/`k.*k`, true); empty strings (both empty true, `mint` with the empty pattern false, the empty text with `q` false); and 300 seeded random cases, patterns of 3 to 6 pieces over `a`, `b` and `.`, with half the texts written out from the pattern and half random texts of 3 to 10 letters over `ab` (195 of the 300 are true, 45 of them among the random texts), checked against a search in the test over every number of copies for each starred piece. Patterns of one or two pieces and very short random texts are left out of the random test, and the seed was chosen, so that no random case is a classic sample, or one edit away from one, even with its letters renamed.

The three timed tests are false at the size limits: `ab` 50 times against `.*b` 20 times and a `c` (the text has no c; the twenty b's of the pattern can sit on C(50, 20), about 4.7 × 10^13, sets of the text's b's); `abc` 33 times and an `a` against `a*b*c*` 16 times and a `d` (no d); and `ab` 50 times against `.*a.*b` 15 times and `.*a` (the pattern must end on an a, the text ends on a b).

Traps, each checked with a scratch solution: a starred piece that must match at least one letter fails the sample, the no-letter test and the random test; a starred piece that takes every letter it can and never gives one back fails the sample, the no-letter test, the leave-letters test, the mixing test and the random test; one that takes as few as it can fails the sample, the leave-letters test and the random test; reading `*` as a wildcard of its own, as in file names, fails the sample, the no-letter test, the repeats-one-piece test and the random test; stopping with `true` once the pattern is used up, whatever is left of the text, fails 6 of 12, among them the sample, the whole-text test, the empty-strings test and a timed test; letting `.*` repeat only one letter fails the sample, the leave-letters test, the mixing test and the random test.

The timed tests have a limit of 300 ms each (3x is 900 ms). The reference, two rows of the table, took 5 to 17 ms on the first (it includes the warm-up of the loop), 0 to 11 ms on the second and 0 to 4 ms on the third over cold runs. The naive solution is the walkthrough's brute force, which tries no copy and then one more copy at each starred piece; it is still running after 3,000 ms (10x) on all three. Its recursion is at most 201 deep, which fits the stack. Java's own `String.matches` backtracks the same way and times out on all three timed tests as well. The walkthrough's Better, the memoised recursion, is O(n × m) like the reference and passes every test, also interpreted (`-Xint`).
