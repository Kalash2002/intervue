# Grader notes: Subtree of Another Tree

Tests cover: the sample, a match spoiled by an extra node below it, a first node with the right value that is not the match while a node below it is, `sub` equal to the whole of `root`, `sub` a single leaf, a value on the wrong side, values whose digits would run together in a careless writing (`[2]` in `[12]`, `[7]` in `[-7]`, `[1]` in `[4,31]`), `sub` larger than `root`, a subtree of about 100 nodes found ten levels down a complete tree of 100,000 nodes and then, with one value changed, not found, two paths at the depth limit (`root` a left path of 1,000 nodes holding 3 except a 4 at the bottom, `sub` a left path of 200 nodes of the same form; only the bottom 200 nodes of `root` match), and a broom: a complete top of 127 nodes with 100 bristles under its leaves, each a left path of 990 3s ending in 4 (99,127 nodes, depth 997), searched for a 990-node path of 3s ending in 5 (false).

Traps from the walkthrough and hints, and the tests that grade them (each checked with a scratch version):
- Writing values without separating them: a version that writes `12,#,#,` and searches for `2,#,#,` with `contains` fails `valuesDoNotRunTogether`.
- Writing values without marking empty children: fails `extraNodeBelowTheMatch` and `sidesMatter`.
- Stopping at the first node whose value equals `sub`'s root: fails `firstEqualValueIsNotTheMatch`.

Depth is capped at 1,000 in the constraints so recursive solutions are safe. The reference (preorder written as int tokens with an empty-child marker, then KMP) is iterative anyway; the walkthrough's recursive Brute force and Better pass every test. On this machine a test thread overflows near 10,000 frames for small recursive methods when they run cold, so the cap leaves a wide margin.

No naive solution: time is not graded separately for this question (ADR 0008). With depth at most 1,000, comparing at every node costs at most n·min(m, h), and the broom above is the worst shape found, about 4.9 x 10^7 steps. Measured with a cold JVM on `broomOfLongRuns` inside the suite (after `deepInsideALargeTree` has warmed the JIT): reference 10, 10 and 11 ms; the textbook recursive brute force 236 and 256 ms; a compare-at-every-node version with explicit pair stacks 792 and 803 ms. Run cold on its own, the review measured the reference at 28 to 45 ms against 233 to 241 ms and 790 to 861 ms. The brute force candidates actually write is 6 to 25 times slower depending on JIT state, short of or too close to the factor of 36 the timing rule needs (reference at most limit/8, naive at least 1.5 x 3 x limit), so the naive was dropped. The limits are a generous 2 seconds.
