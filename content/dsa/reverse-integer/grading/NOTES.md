# Grader notes: Reverse Integer

Same rule and range as the classic version: any `int`, its decimal digits reversed with the sign kept, and 0 when the result does not fit in an `int`. The classic also says the environment cannot store 64-bit integers. No test can enforce that, so the statement asks for it in the Follow-up instead, and a solution that uses a `long`, such as the walkthrough's Better, or the Brute force's `Long.parseLong`, passes. The answer is a single `int`, so there is only one correct output.

Tests cover: the sample (58301 gives 10385; -9400 gives -49, a negative number with zeros at the end; 1563847412 gives 0, its reverse being 4 past `Integer.MAX_VALUE`); 0 and single digits of both signs; zeros at the end (4500, -70300, 8000000, 1470000000, -400, -2000000000); negative inputs (-58, -6060, -864, -2147483641, which reverses to -1463847412, and -1000000003, which reverses to below `Integer.MIN_VALUE`); results that only just fit and only just do not, on both sides (1463847412 gives 2147483641, the largest result there is; 1473847412 is 94 too large; -1463847412 fits; -1563847412 is 3 below `Integer.MIN_VALUE`; 1000000002 and -1000000002 fit, 1000000003 does not); the `int` limits (`Integer.MAX_VALUE`, `Integer.MIN_VALUE` and `Integer.MIN_VALUE + 1` all give 0, and 2147483641 gives 1463847412); numbers that read the same both ways (474, -35753, 1987887891, -2000000002); every number from -100,000 to 100,000; every result from 2,147,480,001 to 2,147,489,999 that is the reverse of an `int`, 422 that fit and 725 that do not, each also negated; and 200,000 seeded random `int`s together with 200,000 random numbers below 10^9, all of which fit once reversed (nine digits at most). The bulk tests check against the test's own answer: the digits reversed as text and read back as a `long`.

The traps the walkthrough and hints name, each run as a scratch variant: checking after the multiplication, with `reversed > Integer.MAX_VALUE` in `int`, fails 6 of 10 tests (the sample, the negative inputs, the just-fit test, the `int` limits, the results around the limits and the random numbers), the same 6 as no check at all; taking `Math.abs` first fails the `int`-limits test, in the string form with a `NumberFormatException` on `"8463847412-"`, and in the digit form, with only the positive bound checked and the sign put back at the end, by returning -126087180 for `Integer.MIN_VALUE`; reversing the text with its sign fails all 10 with a `NumberFormatException`; `Integer.parseInt` on the reversed text without catching its exception fails 6; and looping while `x > 0` fails 9, every test with a negative input. Two more were run: checking only the positive bound in the Optimal fails 5, every test with a negative result that does not fit, and the Better without its range check, a plain cast to `int`, fails 6. Correct variants that pass: the walkthrough's three approaches, the Optimal with the shorter test that leaves out the digit part, `Integer.parseInt` with its exception caught and turned into 0, a check that undoes each step, `(next - digit) / 10 != reversed`, and `Math.multiplyExact` with `Math.addExact`, which took 1.5 to 1.9 seconds on the random test because of the exceptions it throws, inside the default limit of 5 seconds.

No naive solution: time is not graded separately for this question (ADR 0008). One call is a constant amount of work for every correct approach, at most 10 digits whether through a string or with `% 10`, so no correct approach is slower by a factor a timed test could separate. The tests keep the default limit; no approach can loop forever, since `x / 10` always reaches 0. The reference took 75 to 119 ms on the random numbers and 43 to 56 ms on every number up to 100,000 over six cold runs, most of it the test's own string answer; the Brute force took 143 and 82 ms, and the Better 106 and 59 ms.
