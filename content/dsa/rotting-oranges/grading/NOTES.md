# Grader notes: Rotting Oranges

Tests cover: the sample; one minute for a single neighbour (a version that counts one minute too many or too few fails); oranges turned this minute spreading only from the next minute, in rows and columns scanned both ways (a scan that updates the crate in place spoils a whole line in one minute); several sources spreading at once (the middle of a line between two sources); oranges no mould can reach, including fresh oranges with no mouldy one at all (-1); crates with nothing fresh, including all empty (0); the grid left unchanged by the call; forty seeded random crates of up to 8 by 8 checked against a minute-by-minute simulation in the test; and two 1,000 by 1,000 crates: one chain of 500,500 oranges with the mould at one end (500,499 minutes), and every fourth column mouldy (250,000 sources, answer 3 because the last column is three steps from its nearest source).

Each large test has a limit of 2,000 ms (3x is 6,000 ms). The reference, one breadth-first search from all mouldy oranges at once, took 21 to 41 ms per large test on a cold JVM. The naive solution simulates the clock and re-scans the whole crate every minute: it timed out on the chain even with the limit raised fivefold (10,000 ms), since the chain takes 500,499 minutes; on the every-fourth-column crate it needs only four scans and passes in about 36 ms. The walkthrough's Better, one search per mouldy orange, times out on the every-fourth-column crate.
