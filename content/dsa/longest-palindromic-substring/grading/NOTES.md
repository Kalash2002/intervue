# Grader notes: Longest Palindromic Substring

Several palindromes can share the longest length; the statement fixes the answer as the one that starts first, and the tests check that exact substring (its length first, then its text, quoted).

Tests cover: the sample; even-length palindromes (`"xyzzyw"` gives `yzzy`; growing only around single characters returns `x`); ties of odd and even length (`"pip8tot"`, `"xxbyy"`, `"7ZZ7aWWa"`: replacing the best on an equal length returns the later one); uppercase and lowercase kept apart (`"XyYx"` gives `X`, `"Noon"` gives `oo`; comparing without case returns the whole string); digits as characters; the whole string as the answer, odd and even, and a single character; a string with no two equal characters (the first character); 300 seeded random strings of 1 to 30 letters over `ab` or `abc`, checked against a search in the test that tries every length from the longest down and every start from the left; and three strings of 5,000 characters: one repeated character (the whole string), two runs of 2,499 `a` split by `bc` (answer the first run), and a run of 2,500 `x`, then `yz`, then 2,498 `x` (answer the first, even-length run).

The two split-run tests have a limit of 500 ms each (3x is 1,500 ms); the repeated-character test has 1,000 ms. The reference grows a palindrome from each of the 2n − 1 middles and finishes the split-run tests in about 15 ms and the repeated-character test in about 50 ms. The naive solution tests every substring longer than the best so far with two pointers; it passes the repeated-character test, where the whole string is found first and nothing longer exists, but on the split runs every long substring matches from both ends until it reaches the `b` or the `c`, and it takes 8 to 9 seconds on each, so it times out at the limit and at 3x. Two other cubic searches time out on the split runs as well: trying lengths from the longest down, and testing every substring without skipping the shorter ones (which also times out on the repeated character).

The walkthrough's Better, a table filled by length, is O(n²) like the reference and passes the timed tests (about 150 ms on each split-run test); the timed tests separate only the cubic brute force from the quadratic approaches.
