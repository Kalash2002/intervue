# Grader notes: Sum of Two Integers

Same task as the classic version, with a wider range: the classic keeps both operands between -1000 and 1000, here every `int` is allowed and the result is defined as Java's `a + b`, wrap-around included, which the statement says and Example 3 shows. The statement forbids `+` and `-` in every form (`++`, `--`, `+=`, `-=`, a unary minus) and library methods that add or subtract, such as `Integer.sum` and `Math.addExact`. No test can tell: a scratch variant that returns `a + b` passes everything. The walkthrough and the statement say why it is ruled out, and a reviewer reading the code sees it. The answer is a single `int`, so there is only one correct output. A failure shows the call, the expected and the returned value as numbers and as 32 bits.

Tests cover: the sample (19 + 45 = 64, a carry from bit 0 to bit 6; -38 + 25 = -13; 2147483000 + 1000, which wraps to -2147483296); zero operands, with 0, `Integer.MIN_VALUE` and `Integer.MAX_VALUE` on the other side; two negative operands; mixed signs, among them -500 + 500, whose carry leaves bit 31, and `Integer.MIN_VALUE + Integer.MAX_VALUE`; sums that wrap past either limit (`Integer.MAX_VALUE` + 10 and + itself, `Integer.MIN_VALUE` + itself and - 10, 1.5 · 10^9 doubled, and the same negative); long carries (-16 + 16, a carry into the sign bit, `0x55555555` doubled, and `0xAAAAAAAA + 0x55555555` with no carry at all); every pair from -256 to 256; every pair of single bits and of runs of ones from either end; and 200,000 seeded random pairs plus 50,000 pairs near the two limits. The bulk tests check against the test's own `a + b`. The seven small tests have a 1-second limit, so that a solution that loops forever fails quickly. The two bulk tests, `everyPairOfSmallValues` and `randomPairs`, make about 510,000 calls and have the default 5-second limit, because a correct solution through strings needs a large share of a second there: an adder that pads both operands to 32 characters, adds them column by column and reads the result back with `Integer.parseUnsignedInt` took 834 to 945 ms on the small pairs and 346 to 466 ms on the random pairs, and the review measured up to 1,360 ms on the small pairs.

The traps the walkthrough names, each run as a scratch variant: the carry loop on `b > 0` fails all 9 tests; computing the carry from `a` after it has become `a ^ b` fails all 9; a column loop that shifts the operands with `>>` until both are 0 fails the sample and the wrap-around test with a wrong value and times out on the other 7, the zero-operand test among them within 1 second; the same loop with `>>>`, which drops a last carry that outlives both operands, fails 6, the sample among them, and passes only the zero-operand, negative-operand and mixed-sign tests, where every pair either makes no carry or has a negative operand and so takes all 32 columns; doing the Optimal's propagate line before the generate line of a round fails 8; leaving out the final shift by one fails 8; and stopping after four rounds fails 7, the sample among them. Correct variants that pass: the walkthrough's three approaches, the carry loop in `long`, the carry loop written recursively, the column loop with `>>>` that also runs while a carry is left, the adder through padded strings above, and `a + b` itself.

No naive solution: time is not graded separately for this question (ADR 0008). One call is a constant amount of work for every correct approach: 32 columns for the Brute force, at most 32 rounds of the carry loop, five rounds for the Optimal, so no correct approach is slower by a factor a timed test could separate. The reference took 13 to 22 ms on the random pairs and 3 to 6 ms on every pair from -256 to 256, over six cold runs; the Brute force took 23 and 17 ms on the same tests, and the Better 12 and 5 ms.
