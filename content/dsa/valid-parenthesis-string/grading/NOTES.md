# Grader notes: Valid Parenthesis String

The string may hold up to 100,000 characters (the classic version allows 100) so that the O(n²) table of possible counts can be timed out; the characters are `(`, `)` and `*`, and a star may stand for `(`, `)` or nothing, as in the classic. The answer is `true` or `false`, so no tie rule is needed.

Tests cover: the sample (Example 1 needs a star read as `(`, one as `)` and one as nothing; Example 2 has as many stars as `(` but the first star comes too early; Example 3 starts with a `)`); stars that need different readings (`**)(()*)` and `*()*))*`, both true; reading every star as `(`, as `)` or as nothing, the wrong greedy the walkthrough and Level 1 hint name, fails 6, 6 and 5 of the 10 tests, the sample, this test, the long runs, the random test and the first timed test among them); a star that cannot close a bracket opened after it (`(*(**(` and `**(`, false; the single left-to-right count that lets a star stand in for a missing `(`, the one-direction scan the walkthrough and Level 1 hint name, says true here and on Example 2, and fails the long runs and the random test as well); a star that cannot open a bracket closed before it (`*))*` and `*)*))*`, false; the same count run from the right fails these, Example 3, the long runs, the random test and the second timed test); a bracket on its own (`(` and `)`, the forced one-character inputs, false); only stars (`*`, `**`, `*****`, true); long runs of 2,000 characters (1,000 `(` then 1,000 stars, true; 1,000 stars then 1,000 `(`, false; and the two mirrored, which catch each one-direction scan); 300 seeded random strings of 8 to 14 characters, half of them balanced strings with some characters turned into stars, checked in the test by trying every reading of every star; and two timed strings of 100,000 characters. The first is 25,000 stars, 25,000 `)`, 25,000 `(` and 25,000 stars: true, but only if the first stars are all `(` and the last all `)`. The second is 25,000 stars, 25,001 `)`, 24,999 `(` and 25,000 stars: false, because the last `)` of the run, at position 50,000, has nothing to close; read from the right the string is fine until the stars at the start, and no count of the characters alone rules it out.

Also checked with scratch solutions: dropping the `high < 0` check fails 6 tests, Example 3, `)`, `*))*` and the second timed test among them; not letting `low` stop at zero fails 7 with the end check `low == 0` (it rejects `*`), and 4 with `low <= 0` (it accepts Example 2).

Both timed tests have a limit of 150 ms (3x is 450 ms). The reference, the range of possible counts, takes 7 to 11 ms on the first and about 1 ms on the second on a cold JVM, most of it building the strings and warming up. The naive solution is the walkthrough's Better, the table of possible counts, because the walkthrough's brute force recursion is one call per character: on the timed strings it fails with a `StackOverflowError` instead of timing out (and on the long runs it does not finish), so it cannot serve as a naive that times out. The table takes about 7.3 s on the first string and 6.5 s on the second, past the limit and 3x. The same table stopping as soon as no count is possible takes about 2.4 s on the second string, which fails in the middle, and does not finish the first within 6 s; run from the right it times out on both, and so does a table that only scans the counts between the lowest and the highest possible. The two-pass check (stars as `(` from the left, as `)` from the right) and the two stacks of positions in the first follow-up are O(n) like the reference and pass.
