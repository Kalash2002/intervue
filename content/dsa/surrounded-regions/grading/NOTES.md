# Grader notes: Surrounded Regions

Each test compares the whole board after the call. Tests cover: the sample, a corner touch that does not connect (the centre is filled while the open corner stays), a single cell, a single row and a single column (everything is on the border), an all-open and an all-walled board, a region saved by one open cell on the border, rings inside rings (an open ring around a walled ring around one open cell, all filled; then the same with the outer ring touching the border, which saves it but not the inner cell), a region that winds a long way and touches the border at one cell only (a version that saves only the direct neighbours of open border cells fills most of it), thirty seeded random boards of up to 9 by 9 whose expected result the test computes with one search per open cell, and two 200 by 200 boards: one open region of 39,204 cells walled in by the border (all filled), and an all-open board, where a recursive search from the border runs 40,000 calls deep and overflows the call stack.

The enclosed-region test has a limit of 1,000 ms (3x is 3,000 ms); the all-open board keeps the default 5,000 ms, since it grades the recursion depth, not speed. The reference, one flood from the open border cells that marks safe cells in the board itself, took 2 ms on each large board on a cold JVM. The naive solution searches the region of every open cell for a border cell, with a fresh visited grid each time: it timed out on the enclosed region even with the limit raised twentyfold (20,000 ms), since each of its 39,204 cells searches the whole region. On the all-open board it passes in about 2,800 ms, because each search stops at the first border cell it meets.
