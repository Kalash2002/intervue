# Grader notes: Interleaving String

The rules are the classic ones in a card setting: piles `left` and `right` of 0 to 100 cards, a deck of 0 to 200, lowercase letters, and the answer is `true` or `false`. Several splits of a deck can work; the method only says whether one exists, so no tie rule is needed.

Tests cover: the sample (`lime`/`leek` into `limleeke` true, `pear`/`plum` into `plumepar` false, `fig`/`pea` into `fpeig` false); ties where preferring either pile fails (`leek`/`lime` into `limleeke`, and `kiwi`/`fig` into `kfiwigi` both ways round); decks where each pile can be found on its own but not in separate cards (`plum`/`pear` into `plumepar`, `pear`/`date` and `date`/`pear` into `padeater`); the right cards in the wrong order (`kale` and an empty pile into `lake`); a card too many (`figpeas`) and a card missing at the end (`figpe`); an empty pile on either side; 300 seeded random cases of piles of 0 to 6 cards over `a` and `b`, never both empty, half with the deck riffled from the piles and half with the cards shuffled at random (214 of the 300 are true), checked against a search in the test over every choice of deck positions for `left`; and two full piles of 100 with no card in common (a to m against n to z), riffled at random, which is true, and the same deck with two neighbouring cards of `left` swapped, which is false. Two empty piles with an empty deck are not tested: that is a well-known sample.

The three timed tests are false decks where the search has to branch at nearly every card: `a^50 b a^49` and `a^100` into `a^199 b` (the b of `left` comes after its 50th card, but last in the deck), the same with the piles swapped, and `(ab)^50` twice into `(ab)^99 ba` (both piles end with b, the deck with a).

Traps, each checked with a scratch solution: taking the card from `left` whenever it matches fails the sample, the tie test and the random test, and so does preferring `right`; comparing letter counts fails 8 of 11 tests, among them the sample, the each-pile test, the wrong-order test, the random test, the swapped full piles and all three timed tests; checking that each pile is a subsequence of the deck fails the sample, the each-pile test, the random test and the last timed test; a search with no length check, which stops when both piles are used up, fails the sample with an index out of range and returns true for `figpeas`, so it fails the used-once test as well; a search that stops when the deck runs out returns true for `fpeig` and `figpe` and fails both of those tests.

The timed tests have a limit of 300 ms each (3x is 900 ms). The reference, one row of the table, took 3 to 12 ms on the first (it includes the warm-up of the table loop), 1 ms on the second and 0 to 4 ms on the third over cold runs. The naive solution is the walkthrough's brute force, the search that tries both piles on every tie; it is still running after 4,500 ms (15x) on all three. Its recursion is at most 200 deep. The walkthrough's Better, the search that remembers failed states, is O(n × m) like the reference and passes every test.
