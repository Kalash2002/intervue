# Grader notes: Interleaving String

The rules are the classic ones in a card setting: piles `left` and `right` of 0 to 100 cards, a deck of 0 to 200, lowercase letters, and the answer is `true` or `false`. Several splits of a deck can work; the method only says whether one exists, so no tie rule is needed.

Tests cover: the sample (`lime`/`leek` into `limleeke` true, `pear`/`plum` into `plumepar` false, `fig`/`pea` into `fpeig` false); ties where preferring either pile fails (`leek`/`lime` into `limleeke`, and `kiwi`/`fig` into `kfiwigi` both ways round); decks where each pile can be found on its own but not in separate cards (`plum`/`pear` into `plumepar`, `pear`/`date` and `date`/`pear` into `padeater`); the right cards in the wrong order (`kale` and an empty pile into `lake`); a card too many (`figpeas`) and a card missing at the end (`figpe`); empty piles and an empty deck (`kale` or `okra` with an empty pile, true; two empty piles into the empty deck, true; two empty piles into `s` and `s` with an empty pile into the empty deck, false); 300 seeded random cases of piles of 0 to 6 cards over `a` and `b`, never both empty, half with the deck riffled from the piles and half with the cards shuffled at random (214 of the 300 are true), checked against a search in the test over every choice of deck positions for `left`; and two full piles of 100 with no card in common (a to m against n to z), riffled at random, which is true, and the same deck with two neighbouring cards of `left` swapped, which is false.

The five timed tests are false decks where a search has to branch at nearly every card before it fails:
- `a^50 b a^49` and `a^100` into `a^199 b` (the b of `left` comes after its 50th card, but last in the deck), and the same with the piles swapped;
- `(ab)^50` twice into `(ab)^99 ba` (both piles end with b, the deck with a);
- `(ab)^50` twice into `(ab)^50 ba (ab)^49`: every start of either pile has at least as many a's as b's, but the first 101 cards of the deck have 51 b's. The counts, the first and the last card, and each pile on its own all fit, and every end of either pile has at least as many b's as a's while the last 99 cards of the deck have 50 a's, so a search from the bottom also branches for about 99 cards before it fails;
- the piles and the deck of that test, each read backwards.

The first three fail at the deck's last card, so a search that deals the deck from the bottom, or one that first checks the deck's first and last cards against the piles' ends, rejects them at once; the last two defeat both.

Traps, each checked with a scratch solution: taking the card from `left` whenever it matches fails the sample, the tie test and the random test, and so does preferring `right`; comparing letter counts fails 10 of 13 tests, among them the sample, the each-pile test, the wrong-order test, the random test, the swapped full piles and all five timed tests; checking that each pile is a subsequence of the deck fails the sample, the each-pile test, the random test and the last three timed tests; a search with no length check, which stops when both piles are used up, fails the sample with an index out of range and returns true for `figpeas` and for two empty piles into `s`, so it fails the used-once and the empty tests as well; a search that stops when the deck runs out returns true for `fpeig`, `figpe` and `s` with an empty pile into the empty deck, failing those three tests; returning false for an empty deck fails the empty test.

The timed tests have a limit of 300 ms each (3x is 900 ms). Over cold runs the reference, one row of the table, took 2 to 12 ms on the first (it includes the warm-up of the table loop) and 0 to 4 ms on each of the other four; the walkthrough's Better, the search that remembers failed states, took 3 to 7 ms on the first and 0 to 2 ms on the others. Both are O(n × m) and pass every test. The naive solution is the walkthrough's brute force, the search from the top that tries both piles on every tie; it is still running after 4,500 ms (15x) on all five. Two slower variants are still running after 4,500 ms on the two middle-conflict tests, while passing the other eleven: the same search from the bottom of the deck (the table's rule as plain recursion), and the brute force with a check of the deck's first and last cards. Every recursion here is at most 200 deep, which fits the test thread's default stack easily.
