# Grader notes: Edit Distance

The rules are the classic ones: two words of 0 to 500 lowercase letters, and the fixes are inserting, deleting and replacing one letter, each costing one. The answer is a single number, so no tie rule is needed even when several lists of fixes are cheapest.

Tests cover: the sample (`plaster`/`pastel` 2, `sprint`/`paints` 3, the empty word against `fern` 4); the sample with the words swapped; a missing letter that shifts everything after it (`ought`/`bought` 1, `thimble`/`nimble` 2); a replacement that deletions and insertions alone would do in two fixes (`hammock`/`hummock` 1, `lemon`/`melon` 2); two empty words (0) and `moss` against the empty word (4); the same word twice (`velvet`, 0); no letter in common (`why`/`ocean` 5); 300 seeded random pairs of 0 to 6 letters over `ab` or `abc` (answers from 0 to 6; 80 pairs have an empty word), checked against a breadth-first search in the test over every single fix, which is exact because some cheapest list of fixes does its deletions first and its insertions last, so no string on the way is longer than the longer word, and every letter it inserts or replaces ends up in `word`; and `ab` 250 times against `ba` 250 times (2: delete the first a, add an a at the end), where every position differs.

The three timed tests are at the size limit: 500 letters from a to m against 400 from n to z (500: no letter can be kept, so each of the 500 typed letters costs a fix of its own, and 400 replacements with 100 deletions do it); 500 seeded letters from a to m against a copy with every tenth letter replaced by one from n to z (50: each foreign letter costs a fix, and 50 replacements do it); and the 500 letters against a copy with every tenth letter left out (50: the lengths differ by 50, and 50 insertions do it).

Traps, each checked with a scratch solution: counting the positions that differ plus the difference in length fails the sample (6 for `plaster`), the swapped sample, the missing-letter test, the random test, the `ab`/`ba` test (500) and the left-out timed test (449); counting deletions and insertions only, from the longest common subsequence, fails 8 of 12 (the sample with 3, the swapped sample, the missing-letter test, the replacement test, no letter in common with 8, the random test and two timed tests); starting row 0 and column 0 at 0 instead of counting the letters to insert or delete fails 8 of 12, among them the sample, the empty-word test and the random test.

The timed tests have a limit of 400 ms each (3x is 1,200 ms). The reference, two rows of the table, took 1 to 32 ms on the first (it includes the warm-up of the table loop) and 1 to 17 ms on the other two over cold runs. The naive solution is the walkthrough's brute force, which keeps equal first letters and otherwise tries all three fixes; it is still running after 4,000 ms (10x) on all three timed tests. It finishes the `ab`/`ba` test in about 25 ms, since there a deletion or an insertion lines every later letter up at once, so that test is not timed. Its recursion is at most 1,001 deep; a frame of the same shape overflows a plain thread only past 6,000 calls, even interpreted, so it times out rather than overflowing. The walkthrough's Better, the full table, is O(n × m) like the reference and passes every test.
