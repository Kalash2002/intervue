# Grader notes: Word Search II

Every test sorts the words returned, checks how many there are (so a word reported twice fails), compares them one by one, each quoted, with the expected words, and checks that the grid holds its original letters after the call. Tests cover: the sample, words that are prefixes of each other (`ear`, `earl`, `era`; `r`, `ro`, `rot`), a word that can be traced from more than one start and must be listed once (`kk` on a two-cell grid, `ha` and `aha` on `hah`/`aha`: a version that does not take a word out of the trie once found lists it twice), a cell needed twice (`pep`), diagonal steps (`pa`, `mr`), marks cleared after a dead end (`mom`/`omm` with `mmo`, `mom`, `omo`, `momo`), a single cell, a list with no word in the grid, a seeded 6 by 6 grid of `a` to `c` with 300 seeded words whose expected answer the test computes with one plain search per word, and a large test.

The large test is the largest grid, 12 by 12: ten rows of `a`, then `nopqrstuvwxy`, then `bcdefghijklm`, whose cells touch no `a`. The list holds 8,112 fifteen-letter words of the form seven `a`s, a letter from `b` to `m`, any two letters, then five `a`s. None can be traced, because no `a` touches the letter from `b` to `m`, but a search for one of them walks every path of seven `a`s before it fails, or every path of five `a`s when it starts from the end. The list also holds six words that are in the grid (`aaaaaaaaan`, `aaaaaaaaaa`, `nb`, `ymlk`, `an`, `ayx`) and two that are not (`ab`, `zz`). The long words share their first eight letters in groups, which is what the trie exploits.

The limit is 1,000 ms (3x is 3,000 ms). The reference, one walk of the grid and a trie together, took 45 to 46 ms in the test on a cold JVM. The naive solution runs the Word Search search (marks in the grid, stop at the first wrong letter) once per word: 15,343 ms in the test with the limit raised fortyfold, so it times out at the limit and at 3x. Two per-word variants were also checked and both time out: searching each word from its rarer end, as the Word Search walkthrough suggests (13,772 ms, since both ends are `a`), and always searching the reversed word (2,166 ms).
