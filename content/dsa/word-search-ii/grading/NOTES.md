# Grader notes: Word Search II

Every test sorts the words returned, compares them with the expected list (so a word reported twice fails), and checks that the grid holds its original letters after the call. Tests cover: the sample, words that are prefixes of each other (`ear`, `earl`, `era`; `r`, `ro`, `rot`), a word that can be traced from more than one start and must be listed once (`kk` on a two-cell grid, `ha` and `aha` on `hah`/`aha`: a version that does not take a word out of the trie once found lists it twice), a cell needed twice (`pep`), diagonal steps (`pa`, `mr`), marks cleared after a dead end (`mom`/`omm` with `mmo`, `mom`, `omo`, `momo`), a single cell, a list with no word in the grid, a seeded 6 by 6 grid of `a` to `c` with 300 seeded words whose expected answer the test computes with one plain search per word, and a large test.

The large test is the largest grid, 12 by 12: ten rows of `a`, then `nopqrstuvwxy`, then `bcdefghijklm`, whose cells touch no `a`. The list holds 8,424 words that begin with seven or eight `a`s followed by a letter from `b` to `m`, so none can be traced but a search for each one walks every path of `a`s first, plus six words that are in the grid (`aaaaaaaaan`, `aaaaaaaaaa`, `nb`, `ymlk`, `an`, `ayx`) and two that are not (`ab`, `zz`). All the long words share their first seven letters, which is what the trie exploits.

The limit is 1,000 ms (3x is 3,000 ms). The reference, one walk of the grid and a trie together, took 44 to 76 ms in the test on a cold JVM. The naive solution runs the Word Search search (marks in the grid, stop at the first wrong letter) once per word: 18,285 ms in the test with the limit raised twentyfold, so it times out at the limit and at 3x.
