# Grader notes: Reverse Bits

Same rule as the classic version, with the classic Java signature: a 32-bit pattern in, its mirror out. Java has no unsigned `int`, so a word whose bit 31 is 1 is passed or returned as a negative number, and the statement says so. Calling `Integer.reverse` or `Long.reverse` is forbidden by the statement only; no test can tell, and a solution that calls them passes. The answer is a single `int`, so there is only one correct output. A failure shows the input, the expected and the returned value as numbers and as 32 bits.

Tests cover: the sample (37 gives -1543503872, a result with bit 31 set; -16 gives 2^28 - 1; 1 gives -2147483648); 0 and the middle bits 15 and 16; every single-bit word; small positive words whose zeros above the highest 1 must move too (6, 255, 1000, `0x0000FFFF`, `0x0000F00F`, `0x12345678`); words with bit 31 set (`0x80000000`, `0xF0000000`, `0xE0000000`, `0xAAAAAAAA`, `0x80F00000`, -999) and `0x7F000000`, whose mirror has bit 0 clear; words that are their own mirror (`0x80000001`, `0x81818181`, `0x0FF00FF0`, `0xC3C3C3C3`); every run of 0 to 32 ones at the bottom and at the top; all 65,536 patterns of the low half and of the high half; and 100,000 seeded random words, each also mirrored back to itself. The bulk tests check against the test's own mirror, which moves each bit i to position 31 - i on its own. Every test has a 1-second limit, so that a solution that loops forever fails quickly.

The traps the walkthrough and hints name, each run as a scratch variant: mirroring only up to the highest 1, a loop that stops when the word is 0, fails all 9 tests; the same loop with `>>` fails 6 tests on a positive word before it meets a negative one, and times out on the other 3; reversing `Integer.toBinaryString` without padding it to 32 characters fails all 9; reading the padded text back with `Integer.parseInt` fails 8 with a `NumberFormatException` whenever the result has bit 31 set; `>>` in the Optimal's first swap fails 7; and masking before a `>>` in the other swaps fails all 9. Two more were run: a loop of 31 steps and a loop that adds the bit before shifting the result each fail all 9. The walkthrough's three approaches pass, and so do the Better with `>>`, which reads only the lowest bit and stops after 32 steps, and a table of the 256 mirrored bytes.

No naive solution: time is not graded separately for this question (ADR 0008). One call is a constant amount of work for every correct approach, at most 32 steps of the Better, 32 characters of the Brute force or the five lines of the Optimal, so no correct approach is slower by a factor a timed test could separate. The reference took 7 to 8 ms on the 131,072 words of the halves and 7 to 8 ms on the random words, over six cold runs.
