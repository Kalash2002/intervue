# Grader notes: Palindrome Partitioning

The list of ways is sorted before comparing, but each way is compared as returned, because its pieces keep their left-to-right order; a way listed twice stays in the compared list twice, so repeats fail. Tests cover: the sample, one repeated letter (every piece is a palindrome), no palindrome longer than a letter, a single letter, overlapping palindromes (`pqpq`: both `pqp` and `qpq`), even and odd lengths together (`mmom`: a version that only grows odd-length palindromes around a centre letter misses `mm` and fails), and three strings of twelve letters checked by count: the test counts the ways itself (ways from position i is the sum, over palindromes starting at i, of the ways after them) and checks that every returned way spells `s`, holds only palindromes and appears once, so the right count means none is missing. Those three are twelve different letters (one way), twelve equal letters (2,048 ways) and a seeded string of `a` and `b`. A version that adds its working list without copying it ends with equal (usually empty) entries and fails the sample.

No naive solution: time is not graded separately for this question (ADR 0008). A string of twelve equal letters has 2^11 = 2,048 ways, and every correct approach must write them all down; the walkthrough's brute force (every set of cuts, keeping those whose pieces are all palindromes) and the version that re-checks each piece with two pointers differ from the reference only by a factor of about n, which at n = 12 cannot give the 36-fold gap the timing rule needs. The largest test has a generous 2,000 ms limit only to catch a runaway loop.
