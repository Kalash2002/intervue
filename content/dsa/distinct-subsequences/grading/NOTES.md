# Grader notes: Distinct Subsequences

The rules are the classic ones with lowercase letters only: a text and a word of 1 to 1,000 letters each, and the answer is less than `2^31`. The statement says outright that only the count for the whole word is guaranteed to fit, and that counts for its shorter beginnings can pass even the `long` range; only the count for the whole word is checked. This is the rule of Coin Change II and Decode Ways: Java's `int` addition wraps modulo 2^32, so every count in the table is right modulo 2^32 and the answer, below 2^31, comes out exact. A `long` table cast back to `int` passes too, and so does the memoised recursion, whose counts never pass the answer. The answer is a single number, so no tie rule is needed.

Tests cover: the sample (`tomatoes`/`toes` 3, `committee`/`mite` 8, `night`/`thing` 0); a repeated letter of the word taking different copies (`coffee`/`fee` 2, `senescence`/`seen` 3); a one-letter word (`tomatoes`/`o` 2); the whole text (`quartz`/`quartz` 1); a word longer than the text (`kiln`/`kilns` 0); no letter in common (`lynx`/`cow` 0); 300 seeded random pairs of a text of 1 to 12 letters and a word of 1 to 5 over `ab` or `abc`, checked against a count in the test over every set of positions (153 of them are 0, 112 are more than 1, the largest is 42, and in 42 the word is longer than the text); and three timed tests at the size limit.

The timed tests: 300 a's, a b and 699 a's against 300 a's and a b (1: every a before the b must be kept, while the count for 300 a's from the whole text is C(999, 300), 264 digits); 20 blocks of 50 copies of one letter, a to t, against the same blocks with c, f, j, m and r one copy short (50^5 = 312,500,000, while the counts for shorter beginnings pass 2^63); and `ab` 500 times against 500 a's and a b (1: the word needs every a, and only the final b comes after the last one; the counts for runs of a's have up to 150 digits).

Traps, each checked with a scratch solution: running the word positions upwards in one array, which lets one copy of a letter fill two places, fails the repeated-letter test (`coffee`/`fee` gives 6), the random test and all three timed tests; `Math.addExact` on `int` or on `long` throws on all three timed tests; counting where the word appears with its letters side by side fails the sample (1 for `tomatoes`), the repeated-letter test, the random test and two timed tests; answering 1 or 0 for whether the word is a subsequence fails the sample, the repeated-letter test, the one-letter word, the random test and the blocks test.

The timed tests have a limit of 500 ms each (3x is 1,500 ms). The reference, one array with the word positions downwards, took 12 to 31 ms on the first (it includes the warm-up of the loop), 2 to 14 ms on the second and 2 to 29 ms on the third over cold runs. The naive solution is the walkthrough's brute force, which decides for each letter of the text whether to keep it; it is still running after 5,000 ms (10x) on all three, and so is a version that stops once the text left is shorter than the word left. Its recursion is at most 1,001 deep, which fits the stack. The walkthrough's Better, the memoised recursion, and the full table are O(n × m) like the reference and pass every test.
