# Grader notes: Partition Equal Subset Sum

The sizes are the classic ones: up to 200 items of 1 to 100, so the half of the total is at most 10,000.

Tests cover: the sample; an odd total (`[7,2,5,1]` and `[5,4]`, false; a search for half the total rounded down finds 7 and 4, and also gets Example 3 wrong); handing items out heaviest first (`[6,4,4,4,3,3]` and `[5,5,4,3,3]`, true; heaviest first to the lighter pack ends 13 against 11 and 11 against 9, filling one pack heaviest first stops at 10 on the first; Example 2 as well); each item used once (`[2,9,3]` and `[4,1,7]`, false; a table that runs the totals upwards makes 7 as 2 + 2 + 3 and 6 as 4 + 1 + 1); equal items (`[8,8]`, `[5,5,5,5]`, `[9,9,9]`); 300 seeded random loads of 1 to 14 items from 1 to 12, checked against a search in the test over every group; and three loads of 200 items. The first holds 199 seeded multiples of 3 and a 100, with one multiple moved by 3 if needed to make the total even: the total is 1 more than a multiple of 3, so its half is 2 more than one, while every group weighs a multiple of 3, or 1 more if it holds the 100 (false). The second holds 200 seeded even weights from 80 to 100, with one moved by 2 if needed so that the total is 2 more than a multiple of 4: the half is odd and every group is even (false); its total, about 18,000, runs the table close to its largest size. The third holds 100 items of 97 and 100 of 3, total 10,000, and only 50 of each make 5,000 (true); filling one pack heaviest first takes 51 of the 97s and fails.

The three large tests have a limit of 500 ms each (3x is 1,500 ms). The reference, one table of reachable totals updated from the top down, finishes each in under 10 ms. The naive solution is the brute force, each item in or out with a cut when the pack is too heavy; it is still running after 5 seconds (10x) on all three. The third test is a true one, and the search still times out there: it takes 51 of the 97s first and then tries every group of the 3s for the 53 that is left. The walkthrough's memoised search is O(n × S) like the reference and passes in about 10 ms, as do a hash set of reachable totals and a bit set shifted by each weight.
