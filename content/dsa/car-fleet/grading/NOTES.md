# Grader notes: Car Fleet

Tests cover: the sample (2), an empty road, a single car, cars that never catch up, given out of order (3), cars that all merge (including a tie at the gate, `[4, 6]` with speeds `[3, 2]` is one group; a strict-versus-non-strict mistake on equal times fails here), a chain of merges in two waves (hand-computed as 2), the same cars given in a scrambled input order ((2, 13, 6, 9) with speeds (3, 4, 2, 1): the same cars as the sample; walking the unsorted input forwards gives 3 and walking it backwards gives 1, instead of 2), fractional arrival times (gate 30, starts 5 and 9, both speed 10: times 2.5 and 2.1 are different, but integer division rounds both to 2 and merges the cars, giving 1 instead of 2), and two large inputs of 100,000 cars (start positions in a scrambled order with equal speeds, where nobody catches up, and cars all timed to arrive together).

The naive solution checks, for every car, every car ahead for one that arrives no earlier, comparing times exactly with cross-multiplied `long` values; on the first large input that is about 10 billion steps, which times out at the limit and at 3x. The reference sorts once and makes one pass.

Timing (cold JVM, isolated, scratch harness): the reference takes 65-73 ms on `largeNobodyCatchesUp` and 7-9 ms on `largeAllArriveTogether`; the naive takes about 23.6 s on the first. Both large tests have a 2000 ms limit: the reference is more than 25x under it, and the naive is about 4x over the 3x line (6 s) by the 1.5 x rule.
