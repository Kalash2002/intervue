# Grader notes: House Robber

Tests cover: the sample; two houses in both orders; empty houses (one, and three); `[8,2,3,9]`, where both alternating sets give 11 and the answer 17 skips two houses in a row (summing the even or the odd houses fails); `[5,6,5]`, where taking the richest house first gives 6 instead of 10 (the greedy fails); 200 seeded random streets of 1 to 14 houses checked against a search over every allowed set in the test; and two streets at the size limit: 999 houses of 1,000,000 each (answer 500,000,000, which still fits an `int`) and 1,000 houses of 5, 1, 1 repeated with a final 5 (answer 1,670, every 5 taken, two houses skipped between each).

The two large tests have a limit of 1,000 ms each (3x is 3,000 ms). The reference, two running totals carried along the street, finishes each in well under a millisecond. The naive solution is the plain recursion "leave house i, or take it and jump to i + 2", whose calls grow by a factor of about 1.6 per house; it times out on both large tests, also at 3x. Its recursion is at most 1,000 deep, so it times out rather than overflowing the stack. The walkthrough's memoised recursion is O(n) like the reference and passes.
