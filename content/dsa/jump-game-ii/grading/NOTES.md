# Grader notes: Jump Game II

The row may hold up to 100,000 stations with ranges up to 100,000 (the classic version allows 10,000 stations and ranges up to 1,000) so that the O(n²) table from the end, which the walkthrough names as its Better, can be timed out. As in the classic, the last station can always be reached. The answer is a count, so several fewest-hop routes raise no tie question.

Tests cover: the sample, where both wrong greedy rules the walkthrough names take 4 hops instead of 3; forwarding as far as possible (`[3,4,6,1,2,0,1,0]` needs 2 hops, the farthest hop takes 4); forwarding to the station with the largest range (`[4,3,2,0,1,6]` needs 2, that rule takes 4 whichever way it breaks ties); stretches that end exactly on the last station (`[2,1,1]` needs 1 and `[1,1,1,1]` needs 3; a pass that also looks at the last station, the off-by-one the walkthrough names, adds a hop here, on Example 1 and on a single station); ranges past the end (`[6,0,0]`, `[1,9,0,0]`); one station (`[0]`, `[100000]`, 0 hops); 300 seeded random rows of 1 to 12 stations with ranges 0 to 4 that can reach the end, checked against trying every route in the test; two untimed rows of 100,000 ranges of 1 (99,999 hops) and of 2 (50,000 hops); and two timed rows of 100,000. In the first, stations 0 to 99,997 all reach exactly station 99,998, and only it reaches the last: 2 hops. In the second, every range is 30,000: three hops reach station 90,000 at most, so the answer is 4.

The two timed rows have a limit of 150 ms each (3x is 450 ms). The reference, counting the stretches, finishes each in under 10 ms. The naive solution is the walkthrough's Better, the fewest hops from each station filled from the end; it takes about 3 to 4 s on the first row and about 2 s on the second, past the limit and 3x. It is the naive rather than the brute force because the brute force recursion is one call deep per hop and overflows the stack on these rows (a `StackOverflowError`, also on the untimed long rows) instead of timing out. Both fail the timed tests, and so do the other O(n²) forms tried: relaxing every station's range forwards, a breadth-first search, and the mirrored table and recursion that work out the fewest hops to each station from station 0.
