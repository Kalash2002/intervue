# Grader notes: Target Sum

Deviations from the classic version, stated in the statement: up to 60 numbers instead of 20, so that trying every choice of signs can be timed out, and the method returns a `long`, because 60 numbers can have up to 2^60 choices that work. The statement guarantees the answer is at most `2^60`. No intermediate count can leave the `long` range either: every count the table or the memo holds is a number of sign choices, or of groups, of some of the numbers, at most 2^60. The other bounds are classic: values from 0 to 1,000, a sum of at most 1,000, and targets from -1,000 to 1,000. Only one answer is possible, so no tie rule is needed.

Tests cover: the sample (`[4,1,3,6,2]` for 2 gives 3, `[0,6,0,2]` for -4 gives 4, `[3,8]` for -12 gives 0); zeros counting twice (`[7,0,3,0]` for 4 gives 4); a target of the wrong parity (`[5,2,6]` for 4, every total odd, gives 0); targets at and beyond both ends (`[5,2,6]` for 13 and -13 gives 1, for 15 and -15 gives 0, where the parity check alone does not reject them); the mirror of Example 1 (target -2 gives 3); ten 3s and ten 5s alternating for 0, which gives 63,594 (with a of the 3s and b of the 5s signed plus, 3a + 5b = 40, so (a, b) is (5, 5), (0, 8) or (10, 2): 252 × 252 + 45 + 45); 300 seeded random rows of 1 to 12 numbers from 0 to 9 with targets from 2 below minus the sum to 2 above it, checked against a test that tries every choice of signs; and two rows of 60 numbers, each shuffled with a fixed seed. The first has 30 twos and 30 thirty-ones and target 0: with a twos and b thirty-ones signed plus the total is 4a + 62b - 990, which is 0 only for a = b = 15, so the answer is (30 choose 15)^2 = 24,061,445,010,950,400. The second has 1, 2, 4, ..., 256 and 51 zeros and target 77: signed powers of two reach every odd total from -511 to 511 in exactly one way, so the answer is 2^51.

Traps, each checked with a scratch solution: an `int` table fails both 60-number rows; skipping zeros fails the sample, the zeros test, the random test and the powers-of-two row (1); no range check before the group total fails the beyond-the-ends test and the random test with an index out of range. A parity check written as `% 2 == 1` is harmless when the range check comes first, as in the reference, because `sum + target` is then never negative: that variant passes every test. Only when the range check is missing as well does it let negative odd sums through, and then it fails the sample (`[3,8]` for -12 gives 1), the beyond-the-ends test and the random test.

The two 60-number rows have a limit of 200 ms each (3x is 600 ms). The reference, the group count with one array, finishes each in about 2 ms. The naive solution is the walkthrough's brute force, which tries both signs for every number; it is still running after 4 s on both, past the limit and 3x. Its recursion is 60 deep. It passes every other test (at most 20 numbers). A brute force that strips the zeros and doubles the count still faces 2^60 choices on the first row, and splitting the row into two halves of 30 would list 2^30 totals per half. The walkthrough's Better, the memoised search on the running total, is O(n × S) like the reference and passes, and so does a table carried forward over every running total.
