# Grader notes: Coin Change

The amount is capped at 5,000 (the classic version allows 10,000) so that the walkthrough's memoised recursion, up to 5,000 deep with a coin of 1, fits in the default stack of the test thread; at 10,000 it overflows.

Tests cover: the sample; an amount of 0 (`[8,3]` and `[5,2147483647]`, 0 coins); largest-coin-first giving more coins than needed (`[1,8,11]` for 16 gives 2, `[3,11,12]` for 33 gives 3) and giving up although an answer exists (`[6,10]` for 22 gives 3, `[5,8]` for 31 gives 5; Examples 1 and 2 as well); no combination (`[4,10]` for 7, `[7,11]` for 16); one coin, and one value used 1,000 times (`[5]` for 5,000); coin values up to `2^31 - 1` (Example 4, `[2147483647,3]` for 9 gives 3, then `[2147483647,5,3]` for 11 gives 3, `[2147483646,2147483647]` for 5,000 gives -1; a table filled forwards with `total + coin <= amount` overflows and fails with an index out of range); 300 seeded random tills of 1 to 4 values from 1 to 15 and amounts up to 24, checked against a search in the test over every number of coins of each value; and three large amounts: `[4,6,10,14]` for 4,999, which no even coins can pay (-1); `[9,14,25]` for 4,999, which needs 203 coins (199 coins reach at most 4,975, and with 200 to 202 coins the shortfall from all 25s, 1, 26 or 51, is no sum of 11s and 16s; 197 × 25 + 4 × 14 + 2 × 9 = 4,999); and twelve values from 43 to 97 for 5,000, which needs 52 (51 × 97 = 4,947 falls short; 51 × 97 + 53 = 5,000). A table that marks impossible totals with `Integer.MAX_VALUE` and adds 1 to it fails every test whose table has an impossible total below the amount, the sample included.

The three large tests have a limit of 200 ms each (3x is 600 ms). The reference, a table from 0 up, finishes each in under a millisecond. The naive solution is the plain recursion "every coin first, then the rest", which tries every order of handing over the same coins; it is still running after 10 seconds on all three, far past the limit and 3x. Its recursion is at most 1,250 deep on these tests, so it times out rather than overflowing the stack. A depth-first search that tries the largest coins first and cuts branches that cannot beat the best found also times out, on the amount no coins can pay. The walkthrough's memoised recursion is O(amount × k) like the reference and passes.
