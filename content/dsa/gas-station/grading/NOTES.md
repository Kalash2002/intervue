# Grader notes: Gas Station

Deviation from the classic version: the classic guarantees that at most one start works. This one does not; the statement fixes the rule instead ("If several stops work, return the lowest-numbered one"), and the tests check that exact stop. The brute force tries the stops in order, and the walkthrough shows that the skip and the one-pass candidate both end on the lowest-numbered working stop, so all three follow the rule. Sizes are the classic ones (up to 100,000 stops, charge and drain 0 to 10,000), and the constraints state the sums, at most 10^9 each, so every battery value fits an `int`; the timed tests reach that bound (the first timed loop's drain adds up to exactly 10^9).

Tests cover: the sample (Example 1 has one working stop, which neither the stop with the most charge nor the one with the largest gain is; Example 3 has two, and the lower is expected; Example 4 is a single stop arriving empty); the most charge is not the start (`[1,8,2,3]` / `[0,9,1,2]`, answer 0); the largest gain is not the start (`[6,1,7,2]` / `[2,9,4,0]`, answer 2); not enough charge overall although the stretch from stop 1 to the end of the list looks fine (`[1,3,3]` / `[4,2,2]`, -1, which a pass without the total check gets wrong); the lowest of several starts (`[3,5,1,4]` / `[3,3,3,3]`, stops 0, 1 and 3 work, answer 0; resetting the candidate when the battery reaches exactly 0 gives 3, and so does taking the last lowest point of the lap); arriving empty is allowed (`[2,0,3]` / `[2,1,2]`, answer 2, which a drive that treats an empty battery as running out reports as -1); one stop (`[3]`/`[5]` gives -1, `[0]`/`[0]` and `[6]`/`[1]` give 0); 300 seeded random loops of 1 to 8 stops with values 0 to 5, checked in the test by driving the loop from every stop; and two timed loops of 100,000 stops where every stop gives exactly what its leg uses except as follows. In the first, stop 50,000 is 1 short, so the loop is 1 short and every start runs out on the leg from stop 50,000, in the middle of the list: -1. In the second, stops 49,999 and 99,998 are each 1 short and stop 99,999 gives 2 extra: starts before 50,000 run out at stop 49,999, starts from 50,000 to 99,998 at stop 99,998, and the answer is 99,999.

The two timed loops have a limit of 200 ms each (3x is 600 ms). The reference, one pass with a running candidate, finishes each in under 20 ms on a cold JVM, most of it building the input. The naive solution is the brute force, every stop driven in turn; it takes over 8 s on each, past the limit and 3x. A brute force that first checks the total answers the first loop at once but still times out on the second, and one that tries the stops from the last down to keep the lowest that works times out on both. The walkthrough's Better, skipping the stops a failure rules out, is O(n) like the reference and passes; the walkthrough names no slower variant.
