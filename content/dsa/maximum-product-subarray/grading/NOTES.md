# Grader notes: Maximum Product Subarray

The chain may hold up to 100,000 gains (the classic version allows 20,000) so that the O(n²) brute force can be timed out. The classic guarantee that every product fits in 32 bits is replaced by "the product of every run is below 2^63 in absolute value", and the answer is a `long`: every value the brute force and both linear passes multiply is the product of a real run, so nothing overflows a `long` on valid input, while an `int` overflows on three tests.

Tests cover: the sample; two negative gains that cancel later (`[-3,5,-6]` gives 90, `[4,-1,-1,4]` gives 16; keeping only the largest product ending at each stage gives 5 and 4, and fails the sample too); an odd number of negatives, where the best run leaves out the last stage (`[6,-1,3,-2,4,-1]`, 144) or the first (`[-2,5,-1,3,-4]`, 60); zeros (`[-5,0,-3,0,-7]` gives 0, which a pass that skips the zeros gets wrong as -3; `[0]`; `[0,-4,5,-2,0]` gives 40; `[8,0,0,-1,-9]` gives 9); a single stage (`[-4]` and `[10]`; starting the best at 0 fails these and Example 4); products past the `int` range (10^10; 39 threes, 3^39 = 4,052,555,153,018,976,267, which a `double` cannot hold exactly and rounds to ...256; and `[-2, 60 twos, -2]`, 2^62); 300 seeded random chains of 1 to 12 gains from -4 to 4, checked against multiplying out every run in the test; and two chains of 100,000 gains. The first has no zero: 1s, 101 gains of -1 (stage 0 and every stage 7 mod 1000) and 61 twos, so the whole chain is negative and leaving out stage 0 gives 2^61 = 2,305,843,009,213,693,952. The second has one 0 at stage 50,000: on its left a -2 and 40 twos (best 2^40), on its right three -1s at 60,000, 70,000 and 80,000 with 20, 10, 0 and 5 threes in the four gaps, so stopping before the last -1 gives 3^30 = 205,891,132,094,649.

The two long tests have a limit of 300 ms each (3x is 900 ms). The reference, the largest and smallest product ending at each stage, finishes each in under 10 ms. The naive solution is the brute force, every start extended to every end; it takes about 4.8 s and 4.1 s, past the limit and 3x. A brute force that stops a start once its product is 0 still takes about 2.3 s on the second test and as long as the plain one on the first, which has no zero. The walkthrough's prefix and suffix pass is O(n) like the reference and passes.
