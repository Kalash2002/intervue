# Grader notes: Min Cost Climbing Stairs

Tests cover: the sample; two steps with the cheap one first and then last; free steps (all zero, and zeros alternating with 999); a cheap middle step and an expensive last step, where the answer skips the last step because the landing is above it (returning the cost of standing on the last step fails); an expensive step 0, which the first move may skip (forcing a start on step 0 fails); `[1,2,100,1,1]`, where taking the cheaper of the next two steps each time pays 4 instead of 3 (the greedy fails); 200 seeded random staircases of 2 to 12 steps checked against an exhaustive search in the test; and two staircases at the size limit: 999 steps of fees 1, 8, 3 repeated (answer 1,332) and 1,000 steps of fee 7 (answer 3,500).

The two large tests have a limit of 1,000 ms each (3x is 3,000 ms). The reference, two arrival costs carried up the staircase, finishes each in well under a millisecond. The naive solution is the plain recursion that tries both moves from every step; its calls grow by a factor of about 1.6 per step, so it times out on both large tests, also at 3x. Its recursion is at most 1,000 deep, so it times out rather than overflowing the stack. The walkthrough's memoised recursion is O(n) like the reference and passes; at this size its recursion fits in the stack.
